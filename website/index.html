Your card and PIN are easier to steal than you think

Debit fraud needs only two things: a copy of your card and your PIN. Thieves get both without touching your wallet, and debit money leaves your checking account right away.

  • Card skimming

    A hidden reader on a gas pump or ATM copies your card in the second it takes to swipe.

    Gets your card number

  • Shoulder-surfing

    Someone behind you in line, or a pinhole camera above the keypad, watches you type your PIN.

    Gets your PIN

  • Drained checking

    With both, a thief can shop or pull cash. The money is gone while the bank investigates.

    Gets your money
Scam check

Scammers target our parents and grandparents. QuackTag asks why first.

Grandparent scams, fake IRS calls and online romance scams all end the same way: a rushed transfer to a stranger. Before anyone sends $200 or more, QuackTag asks what the money is for.

Coming next: an AI model that reads the full explanation, built on the Gemini API.

  1. Say why you're sending it

    Pick a reason like "paying back a friend" or "someone says my grandson is in jail." One tap, no typing needed.

  2. Red flags pause the transfer

    A scam review checks the answer against common scams, explains the danger in plain words, and reads it out loud.

  3. Call before you send

    To send it anyway, call our fraud line and get a code. A trusted family member gets a heads-up too, and the QuackTag key is still required.

One extra tap, only when it matters

QuackTag adds something only you carry: a key fob that has to be physically present before a risky payment goes through.

Swipe and enter your PIN

Check out the way you always do, at the register, the ATM or online.

QuackTag checks the risk

New merchant? Far from home? Unusually large? The payment is held and the reader lights up.

Tap to approve

Tap your registered fob. The reader shows a check mark and the payment goes through.

Wrong tag is blocked

A thief with your card and PIN still doesn't have your key. The payment stops and you get an alert.

Strong security that stays out of your way

Built for the way students actually pay: lots of small purchases at the same few places, and the occasional big one.

A physical second factor

Not a text code a scammer can talk you out of. The key has to be in your hand.

Smart taps

You pick how much you can spend before a tap is needed ($200 to start). Coffee goes straight through; anything bigger asks for your key.

Lost-key lockout

Lost your fob? Unlink it in one click and register a new one.

Runs on real bank data

Accounts, merchants, purchases, transfers and bills come from Capital One's Nessie API.

Questions, answered

What if someone steals my key fob?
They'd still need your card and your PIN, which makes three separate things. You can also unlink a lost key from your dashboard right away.
Is this slower at checkout?
Most everyday purchases don't ask at all. When one does, a tap takes under a second, which is faster than waiting for a text code.
How is it built?
An Arduino UNO R4 WiFi with an RC522 RFID reader talks to the bank site over USB. Accounts, merchants, purchases, transfers and bills run on Capital One's Nessie API.
