Your card and PIN are easier to steal than you think

Debit fraud usually needs only two things: a copy of your card and your PIN. Thieves get both without ever touching your wallet.

Card skimming

A hidden device on a gas pump or ATM copies your card's data in the second it takes to swipe.

Gets: your card number

Shoulder-surfing

Someone behind you in line, or a pinhole camera above the keypad, watches you type your PIN.

Gets: your PIN

Drained checking

With both, a thief can shop or withdraw cash. Debit takes money straight from your account, so it's gone while the bank investigates.

Gets: your money

One extra tap. Zero stolen payments.

SwampTag adds something only you carry: a key fob that has to be physically present to approve each payment.

Swipe and enter your PIN

Check out the same way you always do, at the register, the ATM, or online.

SwampTag asks for your key

Before any money moves, the payment is held and the reader lights up.

Tap to approve

Tap your registered key fob. The reader shows a ✓ and the payment goes through.

Wrong tag? Blocked.

A thief with your card and PIN still doesn't have your key. The payment is blocked and you get an alert right away.

Questions, answered

What if someone steals my key fob?

They'd still need your card and your PIN. That's three separate things. You can also unlink a lost key instantly from your dashboard.

Is this slower at checkout?

A tap takes under a second, which is faster than unlocking your phone for a one-time code.

What hardware does it use?

Our prototype uses an Arduino UNO R4 WiFi with an RC522 RFID reader that talks to the bank site over USB.
